# Cocoa Atelier: Melbourne market and website strategy

Prepared 13 August 2026 from Elena's four source emails, supplied imagery, current competitor websites, Victorian Government guidance, FSANZ guidance, and Stripe documentation.

## Executive recommendation

Cocoa Atelier should launch as a **made-to-order edible floral atelier**, not as a broad dessert shop. Its strongest commercial space is between mass-market gift boxes and floristry: sculptural strawberry bouquets, flower-and-chocolate compositions, presentation towers, and event pieces.

The best initial model is:

1. A small, fast static website with 6–8 signature products.
2. Direct online payment for standard pieces.
3. A concierge/quote pathway for bespoke bouquets, towers, corporate orders, weddings, and grazing tables.
4. Click & Collect for every product; local Melbourne delivery only for orders of $160 or more.
5. Pop-ups used as measured customer-acquisition tests, not as the primary business model.

No account or customer login is needed. The first site should make buying feel calm and easy, while keeping production complexity under Elena's control.

## Positioning

### Recommended position

**Melbourne's edible floral atelier.** Handcrafted strawberry bouquets and sculptural chocolate gifts, made fresh to order.

This is a stronger and more defensible idea than simply saying “luxury chocolate strawberries”. Many competitors use the words luxury, premium, fresh, and handcrafted. Cocoa Atelier's distinctive evidence is the floral composition: roses, tulips, towers, ribbons, vessels, and colour-matched presentation.

### Primary customer groups

1. **High-intent gift buyers** — birthdays, anniversaries, romantic occasions, congratulations, new baby, and thank-you gifts. They value reliable delivery, a beautiful reveal, a card message, and a result that resembles the photograph.
2. **Celebration hosts** — engagements, bridal showers, weddings, christenings, baby showers, and milestone birthdays. They value visual impact, colour matching, and a dependable maker.
3. **Corporate buyers** — client gifts, staff appreciation, launches, and end-of-year gifting. They value invoices, branding, volume certainty, and clear lead times.
4. **Pop-up impulse buyers** — cups and smaller boxes. They value immediacy and an accessible entry price, but should not define the premium website.

### Brand language

Prefer: *composed, made to order, signature piece, the collection, atelier, finished by hand, presentation, season, arrangement, gift note*.

Use “luxury” sparingly. Show it through presentation, detail, service, and photography.

Recommended lead line: **A gift, composed to be remembered.**

### What successful sites repeatedly do

- Sell by **occasion and recipient**, not only by product type.
- State delivery eligibility and cut-off before checkout.
- Show exactly what is included and how large the item is.
- Make the gift note and personalisation part of the purchase journey.
- Use reviews and customer photography to reduce “will it look like the picture?” anxiety.
- Provide a visible phone, SMS, email, or chat route for urgent and custom orders.
- Separate fixed products from corporate, bulk, and event enquiries.

## Offer architecture and launch assortment

The supplied material contains more than twenty products and many size/colour variants. Launching all of them would create decision fatigue and a large photography/fulfilment burden. Start with a deliberately small ladder.

| Tier | Role | Suggested products | Typical price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Atelier Editions | Entry / pickup / pop-up | Chocolate strawberry cups, small gift box, marble box | $13–$100 |
| Signature Gifting | Core online sales | Love in Bloom, Blooming Atelier, Signature Bouquet, Pure Elegance, Baby Bliss | $150–$360 |
| Grand Atelier | High-value / concierge | Cocoa Tulip, Rose Elegance, Atelier Towers, Prestige and Royal Editions | $330–$500+ |
| Events & Corporate | Quote-based | Grazing tables, branded gifts, weddings, dessert styling, multiple recipients | Quote |

The $12.99–$14.99 cups are useful pop-up products and sampling vehicles. Because home delivery begins at $160, they should appear as **Atelier Bar / pop-up and Click & Collect items**, not beside $500 statement pieces in the main gifting grid.

### Price interpretation

Cocoa Atelier's $150–$500 range sits above much of Edible Blooms and Fresh & Fruity and overlaps the upper end of Lunch Bunch. That can work, but the website must justify it with:

- clear strawberry or piece counts;
- approximate dimensions;
- chocolate type and ingredient detail;
- packaging and delivery presentation;
- preparation lead time and best-consumed guidance;
- authentic close-up and scale photography;
- reviews and customer-delivery photos as soon as possible.

## Website structure

### Launch structure

1. **Home** — promise, bestsellers, how ordering works, delivery message, bespoke introduction, social proof.
2. **Shop** — filters for Bouquet, Gift Box, Tower, New Baby, Romance, Celebration, and Click & Collect.
3. **Product detail** — gallery, price/size, what is included, colour choice, chocolate choice, box/ribbon choice, card message, delivery/pickup, allergens, care, lead time.
4. **Bespoke** — a visual “create your own” path and enquiry form. Do not make customers configure dozens of combinations in a complex product builder at launch.
5. **Events & Corporate** — weddings, grazing, dessert tables, branded gifting, and a quote form with date, suburb, guest count, budget, and reference-image upload later.
6. **Our Atelier** — Elena's real story, process, and Melbourne provenance. Use an authentic founder/workbench photograph when available.
7. **Delivery & Care** — minimum order, suburbs/postcodes, fees, click-and-collect appointment process, safe-drop policy, storage, and best-consumed timeframe.
8. **FAQ / Contact** — ordering lead times, substitutions, allergies, cancellations, contact details, and social links.
9. **Policies** — privacy, terms, delivery/cancellation, refunds/consumer guarantees, and allergen information.

Gallery should be integrated into Home, Bespoke, and Events rather than becoming a dead-end top-level page. Gift cards, newsletter automation, search, and customer accounts are not required for launch.

## Ordering and payments

### Recommended launch method: static site + Stripe

[Stripe Payment Links](https://docs.stripe.com/payment-links) are appropriate for the first version. Stripe hosts the secure payment screen; links can collect addresses and phone numbers, apply shipping rates, accept quantities, issue receipts, and support optional items and promotion codes. Stripe's Australian standard pricing page currently lists no setup or monthly fee for standard payments and publishes domestic-card pricing of 1.7% + A$0.30, with a dated pricing note that should be rechecked when the account is activated: [Stripe Australia pricing](https://stripe.com/au/pricing).

Use one link for each fixed signature product and size. Collect only essential choices at checkout. For more elaborate styling, first confirm availability, then send a Stripe Payment Link or invoice for the agreed item or deposit.

### Important limitation

Separate Payment Links do not create a rich shared storefront cart for arbitrary products. That is acceptable at very low order volume, but a true cart becomes worthwhile when customers regularly buy multiple products or when manual delivery scheduling causes mistakes.

### Upgrade trigger

Move to Stripe Checkout Sessions or a dedicated ecommerce platform when any of these occur:

- more than roughly 10–15 website orders per week;
- frequent multi-item orders;
- more than a few delivery zones/rates;
- inventory or blackout-date management is required;
- staff need a formal fulfilment dashboard;
- manual reconciliation is causing errors.

Do not add PayPal initially unless customers repeatedly request it. Card, Apple Pay, Google Pay, and Stripe Link provide a cleaner primary experience. No customer login is needed.

### Recommended order paths

**Fixed product:** select piece → choose size/colour → add card message → confirm delivery or pickup → secure Stripe checkout → confirmation and care instructions.

**Bespoke/event:** submit date, suburb, occasion, budget, palette, size, and inspiration → Elena confirms availability and quote → customer pays deposit or full amount through Stripe → final confirmation.

## Chadstone and pop-up strategy

Chadstone does not publish pop-up pricing on its website; it directs prospective tenants to a leasing enquiry, including a dedicated pop-up retail route: [Chadstone leasing](https://www.chadstone.com.au/about/leasing). Treat $3,000 as an unverified working estimate until all rent, bond, fit-out, power, refrigeration, insurance, staffing, permits, bump-in/out, and waste requirements are itemised.

### Simple break-even view on $3,000 rent alone

| Average sale | Assumed contribution after ingredients/packaging | Orders to recover $3,000 rent |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $60 | $39 (65%) | 77 |
| $160 | $96 (60%) | 32 |
| $250 | $138 (55%) | 22 |

These figures exclude labour and every other pop-up cost. At a 60% contribution margin, $3,000 in rent requires about $5,000 revenue merely to cover rent.

### Recommendation

Do not make a week at Chadstone the first proof of demand. First run lower-cost, measurable tests at events or markets where the audience fits weddings, gifting, food, or premium local makers. If Chadstone is pursued, use it as both retail and acquisition:

- sell high-margin cups and a small number of ready-to-gift boxes;
- display one dramatic signature tower as the attention piece;
- take deposits for made-to-order bouquets and events;
- place a QR code on every surface to a pop-up-specific landing page;
- offer Click & Collect for the following days;
- capture email/SMS consent with a genuine launch incentive;
- use a unique code to measure later website revenue from the stand.

Victoria requires food-business compliance before trading. Business Victoria advises that a home-based preparation premises must be registered with the local council and that selling at an event also requires a Statement of Trade: [Start a food business](https://business.vic.gov.au/business-information/start-a-business/start-a-food-business).

## Launch marketing plan

### Before launch

- Photograph the final six to eight launch products consistently: one clean hero, one close detail, one hand/scale image, and one packaged/delivery image per product.
- Confirm exact contents, allergens, dimensions, lead time, care, and delivery area.
- Collect 8–12 real beta orders from trusted customers in return for honest reviews and delivery photos—not invented testimonials.
- Create a Google Business Profile once the pickup/service area can be represented accurately.
- Prepare Instagram highlights: Order, Delivery, Menu, Bespoke, Events, Reviews, and Care.

### First 90 days

- Use one hero product per occasion rather than discounting the whole range.
- Build partnerships with celebrants, event stylists, photographers, florists, bridal boutiques, and local corporate-office managers.
- Retarget site visitors only after the basic analytics and privacy setup are sound.
- Ask every customer for a photograph/review 24 hours after delivery.
- Publish seasonal order deadlines early and close availability rather than over-promise.

### Metrics that matter

- Product-page to checkout click rate
- Completed orders and enquiry-to-order conversion
- Average order value
- Delivery failure/rework rate
- Gross margin by product, including Elena's labour
- Repeat/referral share
- Review count and average rating
- Pop-up customer acquisition cost and attributable later revenue

Followers and raw website traffic matter less than profitable, accurately fulfilled orders.

## Compliance and trust checklist

- Register the relevant food premises with the local council and obtain the required event trading documentation.
- Publish accurate ingredient and allergen information. FSANZ requires specified allergens to be declared, and information for food sold without a label must be displayed with the food or available to the purchaser: [FSANZ allergen guidance](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/labelling/allergen-labelling).
- Prepare terms covering lead times, perishable products, substitutions, delivery, safe drop, cancellations, and consumer guarantees. Do not use a blanket “no refunds” statement.
- Publish a privacy policy explaining order, enquiry, analytics, and marketing data. Government ecommerce guidance recommends clear terms, secure payments, protection of customer data, and visible consumer-law information: [business.gov.au ecommerce guidance](https://business.gov.au/online-and-digital/ecommerce).
- Confirm the business name, ABN, domain ownership, and social handles.
- Ensure photographs truthfully represent what customers receive. Several supplied images appear AI-enhanced; use them as launch placeholders only where the delivered product can reliably match them.
- Do not use the supplied images with third-party luxury-brand wrapping (for example DIOR) unless permission and authenticity are established. They have been excluded from the prototype.
